AgroPulse is built around a real problem: farm data is fragmented.
Soil, weather, satellite, and harvest records sit in different places.

yield estimation, condition screening, and resource guidance.

The company targets farm enterprises, cooperatives, insurers, and food

patterns, take action.

====================================================================
4. THE SIX ALGORITHMS — ABOUT 3 MINUTES
====================================================================

[OPEN EACH DEMO BRIEFLY. POINT TO THE RESULT; DO NOT READ EVERY NUMBER.]

LINEAR REGRESSION — "How much yield can we expect?"

Linear Regression estimates a number: crop yield. It uses CYCleSS field
records plus Sentinel-1 satellite summaries, crop, and year. Held-out
R-squared is about 0.66, with an average error of 1.27 tonnes per
hectare. The demo compares the estimate with held-out fields.

NAIVE BAYES — "What is the most likely disease or crop?"

Naive Bayes is a probabilistic classifier, used for soybean disease
screening and crop recommendation. It handles incomplete symptom data
well. Soybean accuracy is 91.5%, and crop recommendation reaches 99.5%,
though that dataset is unusually clean. The demo shows the probability
ranking and the symptoms behind it.

K-NEAREST NEIGHBORS — "What is this sample most similar to?"

KNN compares a new sample with its closest known examples. Our demos
cover soil type, soil fertility, and plant condition, using simulated
but realistic data. Held-out accuracy is around 93%. The demo plots the
neighbours in two dimensions and lets the user change k.

SUPPORT VECTOR MACHINE — "Where is the boundary?"

SVM finds the boundary that separates classes with the widest margin.
In our crop-disease demo, the RBF kernel reaches 93.3% held-out accuracy
versus 87.5% for a linear kernel, because the pattern is not linearly
separable. The demo toggles kernels and adjusts C and gamma.

DECISION TREE — "Should I irrigate now, soon, wait, or hold?"

A Decision Tree learns an if-then flowchart. It recommends irrigation
actions from soil moisture, recent rain, forecast rain, and crop stage.
At depth five, accuracy is 82.2%. The demo shows the exact decision path
and an accuracy-by-depth chart, so the recommendation is explainable.

ARTIFICIAL NEURAL NETWORK — "What disease is in this leaf photo?"

Our neural network is a CNN that classifies tomato-leaf photos into five
classes. Trained on 4,500 PlantVillage images, it reaches 92.2% test
accuracy against a 20% baseline. The demo shows class confidence and an
occlusion heat map. Uploaded images are processed in memory, not stored.
