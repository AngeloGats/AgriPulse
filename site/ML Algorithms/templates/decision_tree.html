          GLOSSARY & FIELD GUIDE

          How does a decision tree reach an answer?

          A decision tree works like a checklist an agronomist might follow. It learned the checklist from past records: at each step it picked the question that best separated the right actions.

          Node (question)
          One yes/no question about a single condition, such as "Soil moisture 24% or less?".
          Branch
          The path taken after an answer. Yes goes one way, No goes the other.
          Leaf (recommendation)
          A final box with no more questions. It shows the action most past records reaching that box needed.
          Split
          The cut-off value the tree chose for a question, for example 24% moisture. It picks the cut-off that makes the groups after it as pure as possible.
          Depth
          The most questions in a row. A shallow tree is easy to read but rough. A deeper tree is more accurate on past data but can start memorizing noise (overfitting).
          Purity
          The share of past records in a leaf that agree with its recommendation. 90% purity means 9 of 10 similar past fields needed this action.
          Importance
          How much a condition helped the tree separate the actions. A condition the tree never asks about has no importance.
          Overfitting and pruning
          An unrestricted tree can grow a question for every odd record. Limiting depth, or removing branches that add little (pruning), keeps the rules general.

          Tip: Load an example, then change one condition at a time, for example soil moisture, and watch the highlighted path switch branches.

          ⓘ

          Interpret results with care. The irrigation data is simulated for this course from simple agronomy-style rules, with some sensor noise and mislabeled records. The tree rediscovers those rules, which is why it works well. Real irrigation also depends on soil type, crop variety and water access.
