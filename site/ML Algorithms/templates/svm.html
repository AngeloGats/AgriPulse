GLOSSARY & FIELD GUIDE

How does an SVM reach an answer?

An SVM does not keep every past sample. It studies them once, learns a dividing boundary, and then only asks which side a new sample lands on.

Decision boundary
The line (or curve) where the model switches from "healthy" to "diseased".
Margin
The empty street on each side of the boundary. The SVM chooses the boundary with the widest street, because a wider margin usually generalizes better. A sample inside the margin is a close call.
Support vectors
The few samples that sit on or inside the margin. They alone hold the boundary in place. Remove any other sample and the boundary would not move.
Strictness (C)
How much the model punishes mistakes. A low C accepts some mistakes for a wide, smooth margin. A high C bends to catch every sample, which can fit noise and mislabeled samples.
Kernel
The shape of boundary allowed. A linear kernel is a straight line. The RBF kernel can bend and loop. Disease that appears only in a middle band of temperature needs a curve, which a straight line cannot draw.
Curve tightness (gamma)
Only for the curved kernel. A low value gives broad, gentle curves. A high value makes the boundary wrap tightly around small clusters, which can overfit.
Distance from the boundary
The farther a sample is from the boundary, the more confident the model is. Beyond a score of 1 on either side, the sample is clear of the margin.
Two measurements at a time
A chart has two axes, so each view uses two measurements. The model can also use all of them together, which is usually more accurate.

Tip: Start with the straight line on temperature and humidity and watch it fail. Then switch to the curved boundary and compare the accuracy.

ⓘ

Interpret results with care. The crop-disease data is simulated for this course. Disease is placed in a middle band of temperature with high humidity, so the curve has something to find. Real fields are messier, and a prediction is not a diagnosis.
