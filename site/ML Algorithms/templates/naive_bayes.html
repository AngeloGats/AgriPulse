          GLOSSARY & FIELD GUIDE

          How does Naive Bayes reach an answer?

          Naive Bayes behaves like an experienced scout. It knows how common each disease or crop is, and how often each clue shows up with each one. It combines the clues you give it into a probability for every possible answer.

          Prior
          How common each answer is before you tell the model anything. A disease seen in many training fields starts with a higher prior.
          Likelihood
          How often a clue appears when a given answer is true. Yellow halos around leaf spots are common with some diseases and rare with others.
          Probability
          The model's confidence in each answer after weighing all clues. All answers together always add up to 100%.
          "Naive" independence
          The model treats each clue as separate evidence, even though real symptoms are often linked. This simplification keeps it fast and works well with small datasets, but it can make the model over-confident.
          Top-3 accuracy
          How often the true answer is among the model's three highest-ranked choices. Useful when several diseases look alike.
          Not sure
          A clue left blank is skipped. It adds no evidence for or against any answer.

          Tip: Use Load example to start from a real held-out record, then change one clue at a time to see how the probabilities move.
