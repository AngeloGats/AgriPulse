        GLOSSARY & FIELD GUIDE

        How does KNN reach an answer?

        KNN works like asking neighbors who have already farmed similar land. It does not learn a formula. It keeps every known sample and, for a new one, looks up the most similar ones and goes with their majority.

        Neighbor
        A known, labeled sample that is similar to yours across all the readings together.
        Distance
        How different two samples are. It combines the gap in every reading, so a smaller distance means a closer match.
        K
        How many neighbors get a vote. A small K follows local detail but can be thrown off by one odd sample. A large K is steadier but can blur real differences.
        Rescaling
        Readings use different units (pH against kg/ha). Each reading is rescaled to a comparable spread first, so a big-number reading does not drown out the rest.
        Distance-weighted vote
        Some models let closer neighbors count more. Each demo uses whichever style tested best, shown in the note under the chart.
        Map view (PCA)
        The model compares all readings at once, but a chart has two axes. PCA squeezes the readings into two summary axes that keep as much of the spread as possible. Dots that look close on the chart may be further apart in reality.
        Cross-validation
        The training samples are split into parts, and each part is used in turn to test the rest. It is how the best K was picked without touching the held-out test set.

        Tip: Use Load example, then drag the K slider. If the answer flips between nearby values of K, the sample sits on a border between groups.

        ⓘ

        Interpret results with care. All three datasets are simulated for this course, with some deliberate overlap and label noise, so they behave like realistic fields but are not measured data. Accuracy on real fields would be lower.
