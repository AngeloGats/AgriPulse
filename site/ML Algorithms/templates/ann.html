    The leaf as the network sees it

    The network never sees your full-size photo. It sees a {{ ctx.size }}×{{ ctx.size }} copy, centre-cropped to a square. Red patches in the heat map on your leaf mark the areas where hiding the patch lowered the score the most, so those areas drove the answer.

    HOW IT LEARNED

    Training: it learns the training leaves slightly faster than new ones

    Each pass through the training photos is an epoch. The network adjusts its {{ ctx.parameters }} weights to reduce its error (the loss). Every epoch the photos are randomly flipped, rotated, zoomed and brightened, so the network cannot simply memorize them. Training stops early once the validation accuracy stops improving.

      GLOSSARY & FIELD GUIDE

      How does a neural network recognize a leaf?

      A neural network is many tiny calculators wired in layers. None is smart alone, but after training they can pick up patterns in pictures that are hard to describe with rules. This one is a convolutional network, the kind used for most image recognition.

      Pixel
      One colour dot of the picture. Each pixel becomes three numbers (red, green, blue), so a small image is thousands of inputs.
      Neuron
      A small calculator. It adds up its weighted inputs and passes the result on if it is strong enough.
      Convolutional filter
      A small window (3×3 pixels) slid over the whole picture, looking for one pattern such as an edge, a spot or a yellow patch. Later layers combine these into bigger shapes.
      Pooling
      Shrinks the picture between layers, keeping the strongest signal in each small area, so the network focuses on what is present rather than exactly where.
      Weight
      How strongly one neuron listens to another. Training is just adjusting these numbers.
      Score (softmax)
      The output layer turns raw numbers into percentages that add up to 100%. A 45% answer is much less sure than a 95% one.
      Epoch and loss
      An epoch is one full pass through the training data. Loss is a score for how wrong the network is. Training pushes it down.
      Overfitting
      Memorizing the training photos instead of learning the general pattern. It looks perfect on training leaves and worse on new ones. Flipping and rotating the training photos helps prevent it.
      Heat map (occlusion)
      We hide one patch of the leaf at a time and watch the score. A big drop means that patch mattered. It is an explanation aid, not proof of the reason.
